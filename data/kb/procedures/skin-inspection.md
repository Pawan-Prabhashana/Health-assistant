# Skin Inspection Procedure

This procedure describes how clinical staff at Serendib General Hospital perform a
routine skin inspection to screen for pressure injuries, lesions, and changes in
existing skin marks. It applies to admitted patients and to dermatology screening
clinics.

## Purpose

The purpose of a skin inspection is to detect early signs of skin breakdown,
pressure injury, infection, or suspicious lesions so that they can be treated
before they worsen. For admitted patients, a skin inspection also establishes a
baseline against which later changes are compared.

## When to Perform

Perform a skin inspection on admission, then at least once every shift for
patients at risk of pressure injury, and whenever a patient reports new pain,
itching, or a change in a mole or mark. High-risk patients include those with
limited mobility, poor sensation, or existing wounds.

## Procedure Steps

First, explain the inspection to the patient and obtain consent. Perform hand
hygiene and put on gloves. With the patient's dignity protected, systematically
examine the skin from head to toe, paying particular attention to bony
prominences such as the heels, sacrum, elbows, and the back of the head. Assess
each area for redness, warmth, swelling, breaks, moisture, and any change in
colour or texture. For moles and lesions, note asymmetry, border irregularity,
colour variation, diameter, and any recent change.

## Documentation and Escalation

Record the findings in the patient's notes, including the location and description
of any abnormality and a pressure-injury risk score where applicable. Photograph
lesions only with consent and according to the imaging policy. Escalate any new
pressure injury, spreading redness, or a suspicious lesion to the responsible
clinician the same day.
